About

Theory that has to survive a flight test

I build control and planning methods for robots, and then I go and put them on the robot.

My PhD work at UIUC's LEADCAT Lab, with Prof. Melkior Ornik, is on reachability: given a system's dynamics and its actuators, which states can it genuinely get to — and how do you redesign the input so it reaches further in the one direction you care about? The result is an algorithm that expands a linear system's reachable set with a provable directional guarantee, by treating the set's boundary as the solution to a family of time-optimal control problems. I'm now pushing those tools toward safe motion planning and actuator allocation on real platforms.

Before Illinois I spent four years at IIT Madras, splitting my time between optimal control on paper and machines in a workshop: a drone-mounted robotic arm for reforestation that earned an Indian patent and flew at the G20 Chennai summit, a coaxial rotor test stand wired for real-time thrust feedback, an autonomous ground vehicle for IGVC, and a quadrotor position controller flown indoors at Inter-IIT.

The through line is that I don't trust a method until it has failed physically at least once. Most of what I know about control I learned from experiments that went wrong for reasons the model had no way to represent.

Perception-aware 3D obstacle avoidance from potential flow

IIT Madras

Nonlinear path-following guidance with a corrector-aided look-ahead

  • Formulated an end-game guidance scheme for online, tight tracking of highly curved reference trajectories, plus a midcourse scheme that routes a distant UAV onto the path with near-optimal acceleration.
  • Improved tracking by over 20% against the conventional constant look-ahead-distance scheme.

Intelligent Systems Research Lab,
Texas A&M University
Prof. Raktim Bhattacharya

How much actuator degradation can a controller absorb?

  • Devised two LMI-based convex programs that maximise allowable actuator degradation subject to H2/H∞ performance specifications; computed degradation limits for F-16 control surfaces and characterised the performance–robustness tradeoff.
  • Designed an LMI-based full-state feedback controller minimising the H∞ norm under actuator deflection constraints, then validated gust rejection in Simulink on two 6-DOF flying-wing models driven by interpolated aerodynamic data.

Pursuit–evasion by pseudospectral optimal control
