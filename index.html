About

My PhD work at UIUC's LEADCAT Lab, with Prof. Melkior Ornik, is on set-based reachability: given a system's dynamics and its actuators, which states can it genuinely get to — and how do you reshape that set so a planner isn't forced to be needlessly cautious? Conservative reachable-set estimates are often what stops a robot from finding a feasible plan at all, so the work is aimed squarely at making those estimates tighter and faster to compute.

Before Illinois, I spent four years at IIT Madras, splitting my time between optimal control on paper and machines in a workshop: a drone-mounted robotic arm for reforestation that earned an Indian patent and was presented at the G20 Chennai summit, a coaxial rotor test stand wired for real-time thrust feedback, an autonomous ground vehicle for IGVC, and a quadrotor flown indoors on nothing but its own camera feed.

Before IIT Madras, I spent my time in Bengaluru, IN, where I was born and brought up.

Warping reachable sets for less conservative planning

  • Developed an algorithm that optimises the A and B matrices of a linear system to warp its reachable set along a chosen direction of interest, making reachable set computation faster and tighter for systems with uncertainty.
  • Showed the resulting sets admit substantially less conservative trajectory planning for autonomous vehicles across a range of road scenarios, permitting tighter and more aggressive manoeuvres than standard reachability-based planners.
  • Currently extending the method to manipulators in cluttered environments, where over-conservative reachable set estimates are the primary bottleneck to finding feasible motion plans.

Nonlinear path-following guidance with a corrector-aided look-ahead

  • Formulated an end-game guidance scheme for online, tight tracking of highly curved reference trajectories, plus a midcourse scheme that routes a distant UAV onto the path with near-optimal acceleration.
  • Improved tracking by over 20% against the conventional constant look-ahead-distance scheme. Begun as an independent course project and developed into a conference publication.

Pursuit–evasion by pseudospectral optimal control

  • Formulated two-agent pursuit–evasion as a multi-agent optimal control problem solved with pseudospectral methods, extracting optimal inputs for both agents under a gamified-health metric quantifying damage.
  • Simulated the full multi-agent engagement within the OCP framework, targeting successful evader capture.

How much actuator degradation can a controller absorb?

  • Devised two LMI-based convex optimizations maximising allowable actuator degradation subject to H2/H∞ performance specifications; computed degradation limits for F-16 control surfaces and characterised the performance–robustness tradeoff.
  • Designed an LMI-based full-state feedback controller minimising the H∞ norm under actuator deflection constraints, then validated gust rejection in Simulink on two 6-DOF flying-wing models driven by interpolated aerodynamic data.

Obstacle avoidance from potential flow

  • Developed a real-time 3D obstacle avoidance algorithm using the potential-flow structure around ellipsoidal obstacles, coupling geometry-based perception of obstacle boundaries with gain-scheduled trajectory adaptation; validated in MATLAB simulation on a quadrotor model.
  • Surveyed 25+ works on obstacle avoidance and perception-aware navigation, with emphasis on real-time, sensor-driven methods suitable for onboard deployment.
